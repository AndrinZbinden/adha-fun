OVERVIEW

What Adha is

Adha is a launcher. It mints a coin on pump.fun and, in the same flow, writes a fee-sharing policy for that coin on chain. The policy decides where the coin's creator fees go: bought back, burned, paid to holders, or split however you write it.

Adha never holds your money. There is no deposit, no vault, no account balance. Every transaction is built in your browser and signed in your wallet. The site cannot move funds it was not explicitly asked to move, because it has no key that can.

The one thing to understand

Pump's fee sharing can only pay wallet addresses. It cannot call a program to burn tokens or run a jackpot. So actions like burn, buyback and jackpot route their share to one executor wallet, and a small service claims each coin's fees and performs the action from there. That wallet is named below, and everything it does is a public transaction. If you only trust what is enforced on chain, use the direct legs: creator and wallet.

What you spend

One launch costs roughly 0.034 SOL with a 0.01 dev buy, or about 0.024 SOL without one. It breaks down like this:

Adha charges you nothing. There is no launch fee, no percentage, no cut of your creator fees. The numbers above are pump.fun's cost and Solana's cost, not ours.

The rent is not a payment to anyone. It is the deposit Solana requires to keep an account alive, and it sits inside your coin's own fee-split account. The launcher checks your balance before it spends anything, so it refuses up front rather than minting a coin and then failing halfway.

LAUNCH

How a launch works

A launch is up to three transactions, and your wallet shows you each one before it goes out.

  1. 1

    Mint

    Creates the coin on pump.fun. Signed by you and by the throwaway mint keypair. Once this confirms, the coin exists and is registered on this site.

  2. 2

    Dev buy

    Optional, and only if you asked for one. It is a separate transaction, so if it fails your coin still exists and you can simply buy on pump.fun instead.

  3. 3

    Fee split

    Writes the sharing policy on chain. If this one fails, the coin is still yours and the split can be attached later from My launches.

The address ending in adha

Mint addresses that end in adha are found by brute force: your browser generates keypairs until one lands on that suffix, which takes around 11 million tries. That work happens on your machine, in a background worker, and the site banks up to ten spare addresses so later launches are instant. You can pause the search from the launch page, and it stops on its own once the bank is full.

Those keys are generated in your browser and never sent anywhere. A mint keypair signs the creation transaction and holds no authority over the coin afterwards.

THE SPLIT

The fee split

pump.fun pays creator fees to whoever is listed in the coin's sharing config. Adha writes that list for you. It reads the existing config off chain first and passes the exact accounts the program already holds, rather than guessing.

The split is written once and cannot be changed

When the config is created, pump marks it non-editable. Nobody can rewrite it afterwards: not you, not us, not pump. Read your split carefully before you sign, because that policy is the coin's policy forever.

Shares are written in basis points and must total exactly 100%. Anything that is not a direct payout collapses onto the executor wallet in the on-chain config, which is why a four-leg adha can still show only one or two shareholders on Solscan. That is expected, not a bug.

Writing your own

The compiler on Adhas › write your own turns a plain sentence into legs. It is deterministic: the same sentence always compiles to the same split, it shows you the result before you launch, and it refuses anything that does not add up to 100%.

THE KEEPER

The keeper

The keeper is the service that performs the actions pump cannot. It is a single Solana wallet:

6xjNfNVyaigQYjLC7vNpUP4cbwHQNNdZhZpreemfvjjT

It wakes every 15 minutes and, for each coin in turn, does two things:

  1. 1

    Claim that coin's fees

    Trading fees do not arrive by themselves. Pump accrues them inside its fee program and someone has to send a distribute transaction to release them. That transaction pays every shareholder of that one coin's config at once, so direct legs are paid by pump in the same moment.

  2. 2

    Spend exactly what that coin just paid

    The keeper measures its own balance immediately before and after the claim. Nothing else can land in that window, so the difference is that coin's budget, measured rather than assumed. The action legs divide that figure and nothing else.

Every burn, buy and payout it makes is an ordinary public transaction on that address, so you can audit the whole history without asking anyone. It keeps a small reserve for network fees and skips amounts too small to be worth a transaction.

One wallet holds every coin's money, so a ledger records what each coin claimed and what its legs spent. A leg that is skipped as dust, or that fails, keeps its share on that coin's books for the next cycle instead of being absorbed by the pool.

What the keeper cannot do: it cannot touch your wallet, mint tokens, or change a split. It only spends what has been routed to it.

LIMITS

Known limits

Things worth knowing before you launch. None of this is hidden in a whitepaper.

  • Action legs depend on the keeper. Burn, jackpot and holder payouts are performed by the wallet above, not enforced by a smart contract. If it stops running, those legs stop. Direct legs keep paying regardless, because pump pays them.
  • The split cannot be edited. Ever. Check it before signing.
  • One executor wallet serves every coin. The money is kept apart by claiming per coin and measuring what each claim delivered, and by a per-coin ledger. It is honest accounting rather than a shared pool, but it is bookkeeping in a single wallet, not separate accounts on chain.
  • Small coins wait. Pump will not release fees below a minimum, so a low-volume coin simply accumulates until it clears that threshold. Nothing is lost; the claim just happens on a later cycle.
  • The ledger is a few thousand lamports optimistic. It records what each leg was handed, not the network fees paid to send it, so a tiny residue builds up over time. Spending is also capped by the wallet's own reserve, so this cannot overdraw anything.
  • This site's list is a convenience, not the truth. The database records what was launched here. The chain is the source of truth, and it can disagree if a launch half-failed.
  • Your wallet can block a transaction. Wallets add their own safety checks and may reject a launch if the chain moves between signing and sending. Retrying is safe.
  • Coins are risky. An adha decides where fees go. It does not make a coin succeed, and it is not a yield product or an investment promise.

FAQ

Questions people actually ask

Can you rug the split after I launch?

No. Once written, the config is marked non-editable by pump's program. There is no admin path back into it.

Do you take a cut?

No. Adha takes nothing. Your costs are pump's mint cost, the account rent, and network fees.

Why does Solscan show one shareholder when my adha has four legs?

Because pump can only pay wallets. Non-direct legs are routed to the executor and carried out by the keeper. Direct legs appear individually.

How do you know one coin's fees are not spent on another coin?

Because the claim is per coin. The keeper releases one coin's fees, measures what landed in the executor from that exact transaction, and gives its legs that number. A coin that has earned nothing has a budget of zero, no matter how much of other coins' money is sitting in the wallet.

If my coin claims 57.5 SOL and my adha is 100% burn, what happens?

The burn leg is handed 57.5, buys with 57.5, and burns exactly what that buy delivered. If the same coin also has a buyback or reserve leg holding a bag, the burn does not touch it: it burns only the tokens its own purchase added.

What if the fee split fails but the coin mints?

The coin is yours and it is registered. Attach the split from My launches. It has not been written yet, so nothing is locked in.

Why is my computer's fan loud on the launch page?

The address search is brute force and uses several cores. Pause it from the launch page, or let it fill the bank of ten and stop by itself.

Do I have to use an adha address?

No. You can skip the search at any point and mint a normal address.
